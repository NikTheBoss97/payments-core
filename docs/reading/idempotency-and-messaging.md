# Idempotency and messaging

Idempotency keys, retries, transactional messaging and webhooks. Part of the [reading list](README.md).

- ★ Butler Lampson, [Hints and Principles for Computer System Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02455) (arXiv, 2021). The long revision of his 1983 "Hints". Section 3.6.2 states this project's retry rule in two pages: give an action a unique ID, remember the IDs of completed actions, and discard redundant retries. It also covers at-most-once versus exactly-once messaging and exponential backoff.
- ★ Brandur Leach, [Implementing Stripe-like Idempotency Keys in Postgres](https://brandur.org/idempotency-keys) (2017). A complete Postgres design for idempotency keys, including recovery points around calls to an external API.
- Stripe, [Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) (API reference). A production contract: saved responses, an error when a key is reused with different parameters, and key expiry.
- Stripe, [Receive Stripe events in your webhook endpoint](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks). Real delivery semantics: retries for up to three days, duplicates, and no ordering guarantee.
- [Standard Webhooks specification](https://github.com/standard-webhooks/standard-webhooks/blob/main/spec/standard-webhooks.md) (v1.0.0, 2023). Signed webhooks: message IDs for deduplication, timestamps against replay, and retries with backoff.
- Jayadeba Jena, Sanjay Dalal and Dmitrii Tishkin, [The Idempotency-Key HTTP Header Field](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header/) (IETF Internet-Draft 08, 2026). The header on its way to a standard: its syntax, a fingerprint for reused keys, and the status each misuse gets.
- TigerBeetle, [Create transfers](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/reference/requests/create_transfers/) (reference). Why a failed transfer's ID stays failed: `id_already_failed` stops a retry from succeeding once the underlying state has changed.
- Stripe, [How events work](https://docs.stripe.com/events/how-events-work). Thin events carry an event's type and the resource's ID, and the receiver reads the resource's latest state; snapshot events carry a copy of it. Stripe recommends thin events for new integrations, and wherever "Data integrity is critical".
- Adyen, [Handle webhook events](https://docs.adyen.com/development-resources/webhooks/handle-webhook-events). Verify, store, accept within 10 seconds, and only then process; duplicates share an event code and a PSP reference, and only some events carry a sequence number.
- [CloudEvents specification](https://github.com/cloudevents/spec/blob/v1.0.2/cloudevents/spec.md) (v1.0.2). A common envelope for events, whose `source` and `id` identify an event, so that two events with the same pair are duplicates.
- Microsoft, [Asynchronous Request-Reply pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/async-request-reply) (Azure Architecture Center). How a long operation answers at once: a 202, a status endpoint named in `Location`, and `Retry-After` to pace the polling.
- Google, [AIP-151: Long-running operations](https://google.aip.dev/151) (API Improvement Proposals). A long-running method returns an operation, which the client polls for the result.
- Apache Kafka, [KIP-98: Exactly Once Delivery and Transactional Messaging](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/KAFKA/KIP-98+-+Exactly+Once+Delivery+and+Transactional+Messaging) (wiki). The idempotent producer: a sequence number per producer and partition lets the broker reject a duplicate and detect a gap, which "indicates that some messages have been lost".
- Lexiang Huang et al., [Metastable Failures in the Wild](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/huang-lexiang) (OSDI, 2022). 22 metastable failures from 11 organizations' incident reports, including at least 4 of 15 major outages at AWS in a decade.
- Sebastian Burckhardt et al., [Durable Functions: Semantics for Stateful Serverless](https://doi.org/10.1145/3485510) (OOPSLA, 2021). A formal treatment of exactly-once processing on top of at-least-once execution: a worker commits new state together with its outgoing messages, workflows record and replay the results of external calls, and caller-chosen instance IDs filter duplicates.
- Malcolm Featonby, [Making retries safe with idempotent APIs](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-apis/) (Amazon Builders' Library, 2021). A token from the caller, recorded atomically with the mutation, a semantically equivalent response on a retry, and what to do about requests that arrive late.
- Nathan Bronson, Abutalib Aghayev, Aleksey Charapko and Timothy Zhu, [Metastable Failures in Distributed Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3458336.3465286) (HotOS, 2021). A bad state that "persists even when the trigger is removed", held there by a sustaining effect such as retries: why features meant to improve reliability can cause outages.
- Michiel Overeem, Marten Spoor, Slinger Jansen and Sjaak Brinkkemper, [An empirical characterization of event sourced systems and their schema evolution — Lessons from industry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2021.110970) (Journal of Systems and Software, 2021). The experience of 25 engineers with 19 event-sourced systems: five challenges, led by evolving the events, and the tactics that meet it, such as versioned events and upcasting.
- Haoran Zhang, Adney Cardoza, Peter Baile Chen, Sebastian Angel and Vincent Liu, [Fault-tolerant and transactional stateful serverless workflows](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/zhang-haoran) (OSDI, 2020). Beldi: fault-tolerant, transactional workflows of serverless functions, which extend Olive's log-based approach.
- Marc Brooker, [Timeouts, retries, and backoff with jitter](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter) (Amazon Builders' Library, 2019). How retries amplify load, and why backoff needs jitter.
- Jon Chew and Ninad Khisti, [Avoiding Double Payments in a Distributed Payments System](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/avoiding-double-payments-in-a-distributed-payments-system-2981f6b070bb) (Airbnb Tech Blog, 2019). Orpheus: a call to another system split into a recorded intent, the call, and its recorded result, with a status query before any retry.
- Chris Richardson, [Microservices Patterns](https://www.manning.com/books/microservices-patterns) (Manning, 2018). Chapter 3 covers the transactional outbox and its relays; chapter 4 covers sagas.
- Martin Fowler, [What do you mean by "Event-Driven"?](https://martinfowler.com/articles/201701-event-driven.html) (2017). Four patterns that share one name: event notification, event-carried state transfer, event sourcing and CQRS.
- Brandur Leach, [Using Atomic Transactions to Power an Idempotent API](https://brandur.org/http-transactions) (2017). One HTTP request as one database transaction, and where that stops working: calls to other systems.
- Marc Brooker, [Exponential Backoff And Jitter](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/exponential-backoff-and-jitter/) (AWS Architecture Blog, 2015). Simulated clients show why backoff needs jitter, and that "Full Jitter" does the least work.
- Collin Lee, Seo Jin Park, Ankita Kejriwal, Satoshi Matsushita and John Ousterhout, [Implementing Linearizability at Large Scale and Low Latency](https://doi.org/10.1145/2815400.2815416) (SOSP, 2015). RIFL: exactly-once RPCs through a completion record created atomically with the operation's mutations, and why expiring those records risks undetectable double execution.
- Pat Helland, [Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition](https://doi.org/10.1145/2181796.2187821) (ACM Queue, 2012). Why messages between systems get retried, and why idempotence makes that safe.
- Pat Helland, [Life beyond Distributed Transactions: an Apostate's Opinion](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2007/papers/cidr07p15.pdf) (CIDR 2007). Commit each entity on its own, enqueue messages in the same transaction, and process them idempotently.
- Martin Fowler, [Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html) (2005). The classic description of the pattern: "Capture all changes to an application state as a sequence of events".
- Philip A. Bernstein, Meichun Hsu and Bruce Mann, [Implementing Recoverable Requests Using Queues](https://doi.org/10.1145/93597.98721) (SIGMOD, 1990). Exactly-once request processing: the client tags each request with an ID and, after a failure, asks which IDs the server last received and answered before resending anything, because many requests are not idempotent.
- J. H. Saltzer, D. P. Reed and D. D. Clark, [End-to-End Arguments in System Design](https://doi.org/10.1145/357401.357402) (ACM Transactions on Computer Systems, 1984). The origin of end-to-end duplicate suppression: retries made by the application look like new messages to the layers below, so only the application can detect them, and a lost acknowledgement turns a retry into such a duplicate.
